# 03 — NTLMv2 Authentication Capture

## Objective

Document the NTLMv2 authentication events observed after successful LLMNR/mDNS poisoning.

The purpose of this stage was to establish that poisoned name-resolution traffic could induce an SMB authentication attempt that could subsequently be used for NTLM relay.

## Observed Authentication Events

Two SMB NTLMv2-SSP authentication events were observed from WINTERFELL (`192.168.56.11`).

| Event | Source Host | Account | Protocol | Authentication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | WINTERFELL (`192.168.56.11`) | `NORTH\eddard.stark` | SMB | NTLMv2-SSP |
| 2 | WINTERFELL (`192.168.56.11`) | `NORTH\robb.stark` | SMB | NTLMv2-SSP |

These events confirmed that the poisoned name-resolution requests resulted in actual authentication attempts rather than only DNS or discovery traffic.

## Capture Process

The relevant attack sequence was:

```text
WINTERFELL
    ↓
LLMNR / mDNS request
    ↓
Responder poisoning
    ↓
Attacker-controlled service
    ↓
NTLMv2-SSP authentication
```

The captured authentication was then available to the relay infrastructure.

## Credential Material Handling

The raw NTLMv2 challenge-response material is deliberately not reproduced in this repository.

This includes:

- NTLMv2 challenge-response values
- Password-derived material
- Captured credential hashes
- Raw Responder authentication output containing reusable secrets

The raw evidence remains in the private evidence directory:

`~/goad-evidence/part-04`

Only sanitized metadata required to demonstrate the attack chain is included in the Git documentation.

## No Offline Cracking Performed

Unlike the credential-cracking activity documented in earlier GOAD parts, no offline password cracking was required for this Part 4 relay demonstration.

The important security property was that the NTLM authentication could be forwarded to another SMB service rather than recovering the underlying password.

## Relationship to NTLM Relay

The `NORTH\robb.stark` authentication generated by WINTERFALL was successfully consumed by `ntlmrelayx` and relayed to the unsigned SMB targets identified during the earlier enumeration stage.

The successful relay results are documented in `04-ntlm-relay.md`.

## Security Significance

NTLM authentication capture does not automatically provide administrative access or domain compromise. Its security impact depends on what an attacker can subsequently do with the captured authentication.

In this exercise, the captured authentication was used to demonstrate NTLM relay against SMB hosts that did not require SMB signing.

The demonstrated chain was therefore:

```text
Name-resolution poisoning
        ↓
NTLMv2 authentication capture
        ↓
NTLM relay
        ↓
Successful SMB authentication
```

No credential dumping, privilege escalation, persistence, or post-relay exploitation was performed.
